| What has already been achieved                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Relation to CRR                                                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Wolfram-model particles**                        | Particles are explicitly proposed as locally stable hypergraph structures that preserve identity; the project discusses topological obstructions and localized perturbations as candidate particles. ([Wolfram Physics Project][1]) | Same ontology as CRR, but I found **no published explicit stationary, massive, wake-neutral recurrent particle**.                                                                   |
| **Wolfram particle searches**                      | Paul Fischer searched rules that keep topological hypergraph defects localized; Polina Blinova separately studied localization of perturbations in hypergraph evolution. ([WOLFRAM COMMUNITY][2])                                   | Very close computationally, but these appear to be research-school/community projects rather than a demonstrated solution to our stationary-rest problem.                           |
| **Graph-Rewriting Automata**                       | Tomita, Kurokawa and collaborators demonstrated self-replication, structural oscillations and phase/synchronization behavior in dynamically rewritten graphs. ([ResearchGate][3])                                                   | Shows that **periodic local organization on a graph whose topology rewrites is possible**. But again, I found no particle-in-homogeneous-vacuum solution matching our Y31 criteria. |
| **Cellular-automaton gliders/oscillators**         | Rule 110 and many other CAs support localized periodic objects on a periodic background; some are stationary, others move and undergo soliton-like collisions. ([Complex Systems][4])                                               | Strong precedent for “particle = periodic localized defect,” but the lattice itself does not grow/rewrite, so it avoids our relational-wake problem.                                |
| **Discrete breathers / intrinsic localized modes** | Exact **time-periodic, spatially localized excitations in a homogeneous translationally invariant discrete lattice** have rigorous existence results. ([OpenAIRE - Explore][5])                                                     | This is, dynamically, almost exactly the stationary massive-particle architecture we have been seeking.                                                                             |
| **Dissipative solitons**                           | A stationary localized object persists because local gain and loss/flux balance exactly, despite continual exchange with its environment. ([Nature][6])                                                                             | This may be the best analogy for solving CRR's unavoidable global relation growth while demanding **local** stationarity.                                                           |
| **Q-balls / oscillons**                            | Spatially localized stationary or quasi-stationary objects are sustained by internal oscillation/phase dynamics; Q-balls use an internal conserved symmetry, oscillons can survive enormously long without one. ([PubMed][7])       | Very suggestive for our original idea that **internal recurrence can coexist with spatial rest** and possibly generate a rest-energy scale.                                         |
